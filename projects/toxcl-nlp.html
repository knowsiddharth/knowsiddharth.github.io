ToXCL: Unified Toxic Speech Detection & Explanation

Collaborators: Huy Nghiem, Sharvani Yadav. Course: NLP.

NLP Transformers Toxic Speech

Most hate speech detection systems focus only on binary classification (toxic vs. safe). In this project, we evaluated and replicated elements of the ToXCL framework—a tripartite architecture based on Flan-T5 that simultaneously classifies toxic language and autoregressively generates human-readable explanations for why a text is harmful.

§ 1 — The Problem with Implicit Hate

Standard NLP models perform well on explicit hate speech characterized by slurs and profanity. However, they struggle profoundly with implicit hate—toxicity expressed through sarcasm, coded language, or "dark humor" where the harmful meaning is not directly stated.

Furthermore, relying on a unified generation sequence for both classification and explanation introduces autoregressive exposure bias (compounding errors). If a model generates a poor rationale, its final classification label often suffers.

§ 2 — The Lexical Shortcut Trap

Before implementing the architecture, we conducted a rigorous statistical analysis (using odds-ratios) across three major hate speech datasets: HateXplain, the Implicit Hate Corpus (IHC), and the Social Bias Inference Corpus (SBIC).

We found that standard explicit datasets like HateXplain are riddled with lexical shortcuts. Specific slurs or emojis correlate so heavily with the "toxic" label (e.g., up to 122× more likely) that deep learning architectures tend to memorize these tokens rather than learning the underlying semantics.

In contrast, the IHC dataset enforces true semantic reasoning by completely lacking these shortcuts. Meanwhile, the SBIC dataset revealed a dangerous resurgence of shortcuts driven by "edgy meme" culture, where perfectly neutral words (e.g., "wheelchair", "oven") were co-opted into high-signal markers for targeted hate.

§ 3 — Architecture & Distillation

To solve the exposure bias problem, the ToXCL architecture structurally decouples the reasoning steps. It uses a standalone Target Group Generator to explicitly identify the targeted demographic before final classification, ensuring the primary classifier evaluates the text with full demographic context.

To robust the classification capabilities of the generative Flan-T5 encoder without inflating inference latency, the system integrates a Teacher Classifier via knowledge distillation (KD). Using Semantic Calibration (SemCKD), we can dynamically align disparate layer semantics, safely mapping the highly calibrated parameters of an encoder-only teacher onto our hybrid Flan-T5 student.
