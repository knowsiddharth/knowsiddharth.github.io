POGS-ACT: Persistent Object Gaussian Splatting for Action Chunking Transformers

Collaborators: Nischay Khade, Parth Goyal. Advisor: Prof. Sanjit K. Kaul.

Robotics 3DGS Imitation Learning

In this research project, we addressed the difficulty of generalization in autonomous robotic manipulation under imitation learning. We decoupled perception from control by replacing fragile 2D image features with a persistent 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation, making policies robust to viewpoint shifts and visual occlusion.

§ 1 — The Problem

Standard 2D imitation learning policies are tightly coupled to specific camera placements. Even slight shifts cause significant performance degradation because learned visual features are not viewpoint-invariant. Furthermore, when target objects become hidden through external clutter or the robot's own body (self-occlusion), adding more cameras scales poorly.

Raw sensor streams fed directly to the policy contain mostly task-irrelevant background, forcing the policy to waste model capacity filtering noise instead of learning manipulation. Predicting actions one step at a time also results in jerky, jittery motion rather than smooth, temporally consistent trajectories resembling natural human motion.

§ 2 — The Approach

We decoupled perception from control via a principled pipeline consisting of three stages:

  1. Perception via POGS: The scene is scanned using a single wrist-mounted Intel RealSense camera to capture RGB-D frames. We distill 2D features from foundation models (Detic for instance segmentation, DINOv2 for robust visual features, and CLIP for semantic embeddings) into a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. This creates a semantic "Feature Field" where each Gaussian primitive carries geometric parameters and semantic feature vectors.
  2. Point Cloud Encoding: We segment the Gaussian map into per-object clusters and extract the relevant object cluster's Gaussian centers as an explicit 3D point cloud. A PointNet++ encoder hierarchically processes this point cloud into a compact spatial latent vector encoding the 3D geometry of the target object.
  3. Action Generation: We use an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) policy. Formulated as a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), the transformer decoder is conditioned on the spatial latent, current robot joint angles, and positional embeddings to predict a chunk of 100 future target joint angles.
§ 3 — Results & Challenges

In our initial experiments with raw 2D camera inputs, we observed significant instability due to visual ambiguity. The robot frequently misinterpreted harsh shadows cast by the gripper, leading to incorrect tracking and poor grasping.

By transitioning from a fragile 2D baseline plagued by shadow-based hallucinations to a stable 3D geometric policy, we confirmed the practical value of object-centric 3D representations. POGS-ACT achieves robust grasping under partial occlusion and randomized object positions on the OpenManipulator-X, requiring only a single camera and fewer than 50 demonstrations.
