Deep RL for Robotic Pick-and-Place

Independent Project.

Reinforcement Learning Robotics Isaac Lab

Traditional robotic manipulation relies on hardcoded algorithms that struggle in unstructured environments. In this project, we developed an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework that enables an OpenManipulator-X arm to autonomously learn grasping and lifting skills.

§ 1 — Simulation & Scale

Training reinforcement learning agents from scratch requires massive amounts of interaction data. To facilitate this, we built our environment using NVIDIA Isaac Lab (formerly Isaac Sim), which allows for GPU-accelerated parallel simulation.

By running hundreds of simulation instances of the OpenManipulator-X arm simultaneously, we dramatically reduced the wall-clock time required for the agents to explore the environment and converge on successful grasping policies.

§ 2 — Contrasting Approaches

We explicitly contrasted two different reinforcement learning paradigms: partial observability vs. full observability.

The Visual DQN (Partial Observability): We implemented a custom Deep Q-Network that relies solely on visual feedback from a single RGB-D camera. Instead of outputting a discrete set of joint commands, the CNN backbone processes the 4-channel image into a pixel-wise Q-map—a spatial heatmap where each pixel represents the expected reward of grasping at that specific location.

The PPO Baseline (Full Observability): To establish a performance upper bound, we trained a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent that had access to privileged, exact state information, including precise joint angles and target object coordinates.

§ 3 — Findings

The Visual DQN demonstrated the feasibility of learning complex grasping policies directly from raw pixels, validating the spatial Q-map approach for unstructured interactions. While the PPO baseline converged faster and achieved higher success rates due to its privileged state access, the vision-only approach represents a more realistic path toward deployment in the real world, where perfect state estimation is rarely available.
