Vision-Based Autonomous Navigation on JetRacer

Collaborators: Parth Goyal, Puneet Kumar. Course: Computer Vision.

Computer Vision Autonomous Navigation ROS

We developed an end-to-end vision-based autonomous navigation system for a 1/10th-scale JetRacer robot. Operating in real indoor environments with only a monocular RGB camera and limited onboard computation, the robot successfully localizes itself, perceives traversable floor regions, avoids dynamic obstacles, and plans collision-free paths.

§ 1 — Camera-Only Localization

To localize the robot without LiDAR or depth sensors, we implemented a dual-branch localization pipeline. We extract visual embeddings from each camera frame using a frozen DINOv2 backbone.

These embeddings feed into two parallel branches: an MLP that directly predicts a pose distribution, and a k-NN retrieval branch that compares the embedding against a reference database. A gating filter then fuses these visual estimates with wheel odometry, selectively snapping to the visual pose or rejecting it based on confidence, resulting in a smooth and reliable pose estimate with a median translation error of just 5.5 cm.

§ 2 — Floor Segmentation & Planning

For obstacle avoidance, we compared zero-shot tracking (SAM2) against a stateless semantic segmentation model (SegFormer-B1). We found that SAM2 failed when the robot approached obstacles closely, as its tracking prompt would land on the obstacle itself. SegFormer-B1, on the other hand, proved extremely robust, achieving an 89% mIoU for traversable floor regions.

The resulting binary floor mask is cleaned via morphological operations and projected from the camera's perspective into a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) via a calibrated homography matrix. This generates a local occupancy grid that feeds into the ROS navigation stack for A* path planning.

§ 3 — Results & Deployment

The complete pipeline was initially tested in a custom Gazebo simulation environment before being deployed on the physical JetRacer hardware in the VISION lab.

The system achieved a 100% success rate in straight corridors and an 80% success rate for static and dynamic obstacle avoidance. The main failure modes occurred in featureless wall regions and narrow passages, where monocular visual ambiguity and limited free space posed the greatest challenges.
